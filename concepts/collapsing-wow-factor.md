# Collapsing Wow Factor

The decay pattern of delight in AI products: at launch you are a rockstar ("we can talk to our data now, no more waiting on the analyst queue"), a couple of months later the same capability is a habit, then merely the baseline — and frustration ("I cannot do this in the product anymore; I saw this other AI product that does this") replaces delight. The wow factor collapses because the innovation changed the habit, and the habit resets the expectation. Now you need to raise the bar again.

## Key Insights

- **"Every time people are happy, you should be paranoid"** — the operating stance: assume what you built stops being cool, and ask what you will show them in a month or two ([[sources/2026-08-30-gtm-ai-agents-lessons-from-deploying-to-6000-users]]).
- **You cannot surf a year of innovation** — staying at the current stage invites disruption within a month or two, because switching to a better product is trivial ([[sources/2026-08-30-gtm-ai-agents-lessons-from-deploying-to-6000-users]]).
- **Frustration requests are the leading indicator** — "I cannot do this anymore" and feature comparisons to other AI products signal the baseline has already moved ([[sources/2026-08-30-gtm-ai-agents-lessons-from-deploying-to-6000-users]]).

> [!inference] The collapse dynamic pairs with the [[concepts/value-maturity-ladder]] as ratchet-and-escape: collapse pushes users to look elsewhere; the next rung is what catches them. The [[concepts/log-taxonomy]] is the early-warning radar — demand shifts show up in the question logs before they show up in churn — and [[concepts/change-management|activation]] sponsorship is the machinery that re-launches each rung.
