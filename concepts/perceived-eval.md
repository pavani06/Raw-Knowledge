# Perceived Eval

An online evaluator class that infers **user perception of the agent** from behavior signals
rather than asking the user anything: is the user correcting the agent, pushing back on its
answers, or trying to guide it in a different direction? At [[entities/clay|Clay]] these run
as built-in [[entities/langsmith|LangSmith]] online evaluators alongside NPS-style
satisfaction scores, in the online/non-deterministic quadrant of the
[[concepts/eval-coverage-matrix]] ([[sources/2026-08-31-inside-clay-s-eval-stack-300m-agent-runs-one-langsmith-pipel]]).

Where [[concepts/llm-as-judge]] grades the output against a rubric, perceived evals grade
the *conversation's effect on the user*: corrections and pushback are implicit negative
labels generated by real usage, at zero survey cost.

## Key Insights

- **Corrections are labels** — a user rewriting the agent's answer or steering it back on
  course is a grounded quality signal from production traffic, harvestable at scale
  ([[sources/2026-08-31-inside-clay-s-eval-stack-300m-agent-runs-one-langsmith-pipel]]).
- **Perceived evals feed the drift loop** — examples pulled from online evaluators
  (perceived eval included) are one of Clay's countermeasures against eval drift; the
  perception signal becomes offline eval material (see
  [[concepts/production-to-offline-feedback-loop]])
  ([[sources/2026-08-31-inside-clay-s-eval-stack-300m-agent-runs-one-langsmith-pipel]]).

> [!inference] Perceived eval is the implicit-feedback complement to explicit satisfaction
  metrics (NPS/CSAT): CSAT tells you how users felt, corrections tell you *what* was wrong.
  It generalizes the CSAT-drop-detection pattern from
  [[concepts/continuous-evaluation]] into an always-on behavioral signal.
