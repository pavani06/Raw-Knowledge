# Balancing Feedback

A feedback loop that counteracts change and drives a system toward a goal or equilibrium. One of the two fundamental loop types in [[system-dynamics]] and [[causal-loop-diagrams]], alongside [[reinforcing-feedback]].

In a balancing loop, an action produces a result that feeds back to reduce the gap between the current state and the goal — stabilizing the system around a target.

## Key Insights

- **Core mechanism**: a gap between desired and actual state drives corrective action that reduces the gap ([[sources/2026-06-25-systems-thinking-for-leaders-designing-solutions-that-work]])
- **Everyday example**: riding a bicycle — at every instant, the rider compares the desired path against the actual trajectory and adjusts the handlebars to close the gap. Without this balancing feedback, the rider crashes immediately
- **Organizational example**: in the healthcare case study, the intended effect of prior approval is a balancing loop: pressure to cut costs → tighter restrictions → lower unit cost of drugs/procedures → lower total costs → reduced pressure. This loop is real and works in the short run, which is why decision-makers believe the policy is effective
- **[[policy-resistance]] trap**: balancing loops that appear to work in the short term mask the [[reinforcing-feedback]] vicious cycles that operate through long time delays and eventually dominate. The short-term success of the balancing loop powerfully reinforces the wrong mental model
