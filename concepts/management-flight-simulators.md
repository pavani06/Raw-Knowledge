# Management Flight Simulators

Interactive computer simulation environments that let managers and leaders experiment with complex system strategies in a risk-free setting. Built on [[system-dynamics]] models, they serve as the primary experiential learning tool of [[systems-thinking]].

The analogy to aviation flight simulators is deliberate: "You do not want to get on any commercial flight unless those pilots have had significant simulator training so that they know what to do if there is an emergency without having to go, 'Oh, I have never experienced this before. Now what do I do?' Because there is not going to be time for that." ([[sources/2026-06-25-systems-thinking-for-leaders-designing-solutions-that-work]])

## Key Insights

- **Why simulators over lectures**: "Research shows that showing people research does not work." Telling people the optimal strategy does not change mental models — they need to discover it through direct experience in the safe space the simulator provides ([[sources/2026-06-25-systems-thinking-for-leaders-designing-solutions-that-work]])
- **Project management demo**: Sterman demonstrates a project management simulator where he manages a hardware development project — starts with 70 people, accepts scope creep from marketing, applies management pressure as deadlines slip, hires frantically, and ends with a $50M loss, 25% defect rate, and massive schedule overrun — a "pretty horrible project" that mirrors real outcomes
- **Climate policy application**: [[en-roads|En-ROADS]], developed with [[climate-interactive|Climate Interactive]], is a management flight simulator for climate policy. Users can change model assumptions (energy prices, technology costs, climate sensitivity) and explore how different policy portfolios affect global temperature
- **Design philosophy**: simulators should let users challenge the assumptions, not just provide "the answer." "All models are wrong. Some models are useful" (George Box). The learning happens when the user's mental model collides with the simulator's behavior
