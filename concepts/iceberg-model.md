# Iceberg Model

A framing metaphor in [[systems-thinking]] that distinguishes between visible system elements (above the waterline — what managers perceive and react to) and hidden system structure (below the waterline — the feedback loops, time delays, and unintended consequences that determine long-term outcomes).

The iceberg model explains why [[policy-resistance]] is so persistent: decision-makers respond to what they can see (the intended short-term effects of their actions) while the consequences they cannot see (the [[reinforcing-feedback]] vicious cycles operating through long delays) accumulate below the surface.

## Key Insights

- **Above the waterline**: in the healthcare case study, managers see the [[balancing-feedback]] loop — tighter prior approval → lower unit drug costs → lower total costs. This is visible, measurable, and appears to confirm the policy is working ([[sources/2026-06-25-systems-thinking-for-leaders-designing-solutions-that-work]])
- **Below the waterline**: five [[reinforcing-feedback]] vicious cycles operate invisibly — care deterioration leading to more visits, administrative appeals, emergency escalation, readmissions, and patient attrition. These take months or years to surface as cost increases
- **The challenge**: "always challenge the boundary of your model" — what appears to be external (revenue, market conditions) is often part of the system when you look below the waterline. In the healthcare case, revenue was initially treated as external until the vicious cycle of patient attrition was mapped
- The iceberg model pairs with [[causal-loop-diagrams]]: causal mapping is the technique that reveals what is below the waterline
