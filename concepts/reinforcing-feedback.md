# Reinforcing Feedback

A feedback loop that amplifies change in the direction it is already moving — either vicious cycles that make problems worse or virtuous cycles that accelerate improvement. One of the two fundamental loop types in [[system-dynamics]] and [[causal-loop-diagrams]], alongside [[balancing-feedback]].

In a reinforcing loop, an initial change produces effects that feed back to drive further change in the same direction. Once set in motion, the cycle compounds — making it both powerful and dangerous when it operates as a vicious cycle.

## Key Insights

- **[[policy-resistance]] mechanism**: reinforcing feedback is why well-intentioned solutions fail. The healthcare prior-approval case study maps five distinct vicious cycles ([[sources/2026-06-25-systems-thinking-for-leaders-designing-solutions-that-work]]):
  1. Tighter restrictions → worse care → more doctor visits and tests → higher total costs → tighter restrictions
  2. Worse care → administrative appeals → higher administrative costs → tighter restrictions
  3. Worse care → emergency department visits → hospitalizations → higher costs → tighter restrictions
  4. Worse care → hospital readmissions → higher costs → tighter restrictions
  5. Worse care → patient dissatisfaction → patients switch plans → lower revenue → financial pressure → tighter restrictions
- **Compounding nature**: unlike [[balancing-feedback]], reinforcing loops have no natural limit — they can spiral until something external intervenes or the system collapses
- **Time delays mask them**: the vicious cycles operate through long delays (e.g., health deterioration takes months, lawsuits take years), so the connection between the initial policy decision and the eventual cost increase is invisible to decision-makers
