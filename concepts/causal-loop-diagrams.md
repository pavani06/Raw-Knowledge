# Causal Loop Diagrams

A diagramming technique for mapping how elements in a system influence each other through feedback loops. The primary qualitative tool of [[systems-thinking]], used to diagnose [[policy-resistance]] by making hidden feedback structures visible.

A causal loop diagram shows variables connected by causal links marked with polarity (+ or -): a "+" means the variables move in the same direction (more A → more B), a "-" means they move in opposite directions (more A → less B). Loops are classified as [[balancing-feedback]] (self-correcting, goal-seeking) or [[reinforcing-feedback]] (self-amplifying, vicious or virtuous cycles).

## Key Insights

- Causal mapping is effective as a **qualitative-only** tool — significant value can be delivered without building a formal simulation model, by expanding the diversity of stakeholders in the mapping process ([[sources/2026-06-25-systems-thinking-for-leaders-designing-solutions-that-work]])
- The [[iceberg-model]] frames what causal mapping reveals: the intended effects (above the waterline) are what managers see; the unintended consequences (below the waterline) are what mapping exposes
- In the healthcare [[policy-resistance]] case study, Sterman maps five distinct [[reinforcing-feedback]] vicious cycles that operate below the waterline of prior approval policies: care deterioration → more visits/tests, administrative appeals → higher costs, emergency department escalation, hospital readmissions, and litigation
