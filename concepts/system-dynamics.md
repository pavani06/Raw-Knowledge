# System Dynamics

A methodology for modeling and simulating complex systems developed by Jay W. Forrester at MIT in the 1950s. System dynamics uses stocks, flows, feedback loops, and time delays to understand how system structure produces behavior over time.

> [!inference] System dynamics is the formal, often quantitative parent discipline of [[systems-thinking]]. While systems thinking emphasizes the qualitative mental models and causal mapping, system dynamics adds computer simulation to test those models rigorously.

## Key Insights

- Originated at MIT Sloan under Jay W. Forrester, the foundational figure for both [[systems-thinking]] and [[management-flight-simulators]] ([[sources/2026-06-25-systems-thinking-for-leaders-designing-solutions-that-work]])
- Defines two fundamental feedback types: [[balancing-feedback]] (goal-seeking, stabilizing) and [[reinforcing-feedback]] (vicious/virtuous cycles, amplifying)
- Applied across domains: corporate strategy, sustainability, public policy, climate change, healthcare, project management, energy policy
- Underlies the development of [[management-flight-simulators]] — interactive computer models that let managers experiment with system behavior in a risk-free environment
