# Group Modeling Process

A facilitated method for building shared understanding of a complex system by bringing diverse stakeholders together to collaboratively map its structure. A core qualitative practice within [[systems-thinking]].

The central principle: "you want the system in the room." Include all key actors — even traditional adversaries such as consumer groups or upstream supply chain partners — so that collectively the group can identify the so-called side effects that have caused past initiatives to fail ([[sources/2026-06-25-systems-thinking-for-leaders-designing-solutions-that-work]]).

## Key Insights

- **Two levels of value**: (1) **substantive** — deeper shared understanding of what is driving system behavior, exposing the [[reinforcing-feedback]] vicious cycles hidden in the [[iceberg-model]]; (2) **meta/capability-building** — participants develop systems thinking skills including listening before concluding, collaborative work, and intellectual humility ([[sources/2026-06-25-systems-thinking-for-leaders-designing-solutions-that-work]])
- **Facilitator stance**: the role shifts from "sage on the stage" (the expert who tells people the answer) to "guide on the side" (the catalyst who helps the group discover the answer). "I am not the expert. I am here to help catalyze your learning"
- **Qualitative tool**: does not require building a formal simulation model. [[causal-loop-diagrams]] alone, developed collaboratively, deliver significant value
- **Implementation reality**: the executive program devotes a full day to practitioners from business and other sectors presenting how they actually implemented these tools — not the theory but the "real issues that come up when people are very, very busy and do not have a PhD from MIT in system dynamics"
