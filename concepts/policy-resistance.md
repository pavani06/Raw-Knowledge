# Policy Resistance

The phenomenon where a well-researched, expertly-designed solution to a pressing problem succeeds in the near term but the problem returns later — often worse. The defining problem that [[systems-thinking]] exists to address.

Policy resistance is not the inability to get a solution implemented. It is more insidious: you do all the research, consult all the experts, build consensus, get the solution implemented — and it either does not work, or works temporarily before the problem comes back through unintended consequences.

## Key Insights

- **Root cause**: the mismatch between the [[open-loop-mental-model]] decision-makers use and the feedback complexity of real systems. Every decision has multiple effects; the ones that feed back and undermine goals are dismissed as "side effects" ([[sources/2026-06-25-systems-thinking-for-leaders-designing-solutions-that-work]])
- **Mechanism**: [[balancing-feedback]] loops that were intended to control the problem are counteracted by [[reinforcing-feedback]] loops — vicious cycles triggered by the very actions taken to solve it
- **Historical depth**: described 500 years ago by Sir Thomas More in *Utopia*: "by applying a remedy to one sore, you provoke another; and what removes one ill symptom produces others"
- **Healthcare case**: prior approval, preferred drug lists, and step therapy were designed to cut costs by restricting expensive drugs. Instead, they delay/deny appropriate care → worse health outcomes → more doctor visits, tests, hospitalizations → higher total costs → tighter restrictions → vicious cycle. Estimated $35B/year in US administrative costs alone (2024). Studies going back to 1996 show these policies *increase* total medical costs ([[sources/2026-06-25-systems-thinking-for-leaders-designing-solutions-that-work]])
- **Traffic case**: building more roads to reduce congestion makes driving more attractive → more driving, less transit use, farther commutes → congestion returns or worsens
- **Project management case**: the common pattern of late, over-budget, low-quality projects
